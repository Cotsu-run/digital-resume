The brief and the build

What was needed

Every revision had to be checked against the approved file, and by hand that is slow. What slips through is rarely the obvious thing — it is the small change that carries the most risk once the job is printed.

Work I did

I shaped the product and built it: framing the problem, setting what the system has to catch, and designing how a reviewer works through what it finds. I then implemented it as a Next.js web app that keeps the artwork and its checks in one workspace, in Thai and English.

The workspace

The artwork on the left, everything found on it on the right. Pick a row and the viewer flies to that spot; pick a box and the list follows.

ruler · results

Font size, measured

Each text run is sized from its glyphs — Thai by body height, Latin by cap height — and given in millimetres and points.

Line weights, measured

Every horizontal rule is found and its thickness measured below the pixel, so a 0.18 mm hairline and a 0.35 mm rule are told apart.

A record that ships

The list, the overlay and every export come from one set of findings — JSON, CSV, or a PDF report with the marked-up artwork.

Measured, not eyeballed

Known weights in, measured weights out

The test label is drawn in code, so the true weight of every rule is known. By eye they are all “a thin line”; Ruler reads each one to within 0.002 mm.
